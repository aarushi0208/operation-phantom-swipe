# 1. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Operation Phantom Swipe is a simulated digital forensics
investigation involving an alleged cross-border financial fraud
ring.

The simulated investigation focuses on three primary cybercrime
activities:

1. ATM skimming
2. Credit-card cloning
3. Online credit-card fraud

Two simulated devices were considered during the investigation:

- Device 01: ATM skimmer
- Device 02: Mobile phone containing fraudulent applications and
  communication records

The investigation followed basic digital-forensic principles,
including evidence preservation, SHA-256 hashing, chain of custody,
forensic searching and controlled password-recovery testing.

All evidence used in this project is synthetic and was created
specifically for academic demonstration. No real financial,
personal or confidential information was used.

---

# 2. OBJECTIVES

The main objectives of the investigation were:

- To identify and classify the cybercrimes involved.
- To simulate the seizure of electronic evidence.
- To maintain a documented chain of custody.
- To calculate SHA-256 hashes for acquired evidence.
- To search simulated devices for relevant digital artefacts.
- To demonstrate metadata and string-based evidence analysis.
- To perform a controlled dictionary-based password recovery
  simulation.
- To examine legal and ethical considerations.
- To understand challenges associated with cross-border
  investigations.

# 8. HASHING AND EVIDENCE INTEGRITY

SHA-256 was used to calculate cryptographic hashes for the
simulated evidence.

The hashing script is:

`scripts/hash_files.py`

The resulting hashes are stored in:

`acquisition/hashes_sha256.txt`

A cryptographic hash provides a fixed-length representation of
digital data.

If the contents of a file change, its SHA-256 hash will normally
change.

Therefore, hash comparison can help investigators verify that a
forensic copy has not been modified.

# 11. CRYPTOGRAPHY AND PASSWORD RECOVERY

A controlled password-recovery experiment was performed using
synthetic evidence.

The password:

`phantom2026`

was converted into a SHA-256 hash.

A dictionary containing possible passwords was then tested.

For each candidate password, its SHA-256 hash was calculated and
compared with the target hash.

The matching candidate was successfully identified.

This demonstrated the basic principle of a dictionary attack.

---

# 12. ETHICAL CONSIDERATIONS

Password recovery must only be performed when investigators have
appropriate legal authority.

Investigators should not attempt to access real devices,
accounts or data without authorization.

In this assignment, all password recovery was performed against
synthetic data created specifically for educational purposes.

The experiment demonstrates a forensic technique without accessing
real-world systems.

---

# 13. PASSWORD SECURITY OBSERVATION

The simulated password demonstrates the risks associated with
predictable passwords.

Passwords containing common words, names or easily predictable
numbers can be more vulnerable to dictionary-based attacks.

Users should instead use:

- Long passwords
- Unique passwords
- Password managers
- Multi-factor authentication
- Avoidance of predictable patterns

---

# 18. RECOMMENDATIONS FOR LAW ENFORCEMENT SOPs

The following improvements are recommended:

### 1. Standardized Acquisition

Use documented procedures for seizure and forensic imaging.

### 2. Write Protection

Use appropriate hardware or software write-protection methods when
handling original storage media.

### 3. Cryptographic Verification

Calculate SHA-256 or other approved cryptographic hashes during
acquisition and verification.

### 4. Detailed Chain of Custody

Record every evidence transfer, examination and storage event.

### 5. Automated Evidence Logging

Use standardized tools to record file paths, timestamps and hash
values.

### 6. Cross-Border Coordination

Establish rapid communication procedures with international
counterparts and relevant service providers.

### 7. Evidence Preservation

Request preservation of relevant electronic information as early
as legally permitted.

### 8. Examiner Training

Regularly train investigators in mobile, cloud, network and
financial forensics.
